## What I do

- **Security engineering:** Threat modelling, vulnerability remediation, secure SDLC, SAST/DAST, and the occasional uncomfortable conversation about risk.
- **Malware analysis:** Investigating suspicious binaries and behaviour to understand what a sample does, how it operates, and what it might compromise.
- **Firmware analysis:** Looking beneath the software layer to examine embedded systems, firmware behaviour, and potential security weaknesses. The device says it's fine. The device has not been peer-reviewed.
- **Bug bounty and vulnerability research:** Hunting for security flaws, validating findings, and responsibly reporting vulnerabilities. Breaking things is considerably more useful when you can explain why they broke.
- **Cloud and infrastructure:** AWS, Linux, IAM, hardening, Docker, and making misconfigured infrastructure slightly less enthusiastic about being compromised.
- **Detection and response:** CrowdStrike Falcon SIEM/XDR, IOC automation, incident-response planning, and SOC operations.
- **Software engineering:** Python, Go, TypeScript, JavaScript, backend systems, APIs, and automation.
- **Security and compliance:** ISO 27001, GDPR, security assessments, and helping technical reality coexist with compliance requirements.

## Security research

Not every security problem comes with a dashboard, a vendor, or a conveniently worded alert.

I also spend time on the more investigative side of security:

- **Malware analysis:** Understanding suspicious code, execution behaviour, and potential indicators of compromise.
- **Firmware analysis:** Investigating the software embedded in devices, where security assumptions can get particularly creative.
- **Bug bounty:** Researching vulnerabilities in authorised targets, validating security impact, and helping turn findings into actionable fixes.

The goal isn't to call everything a vulnerability. It's to understand what actually happens, demonstrate the risk, and produce evidence that survives contact with someone asking, "Can you reproduce that?"
